## Why do people question whether OKX is legit?

Searches for “is OKX legit” usually come from one of four concerns.

### 1. Confusion about regulation

Crypto exchanges rarely have one universal license that covers the entire world. The legal entity serving a customer in the United States may be different from the entity serving a customer in Europe, Singapore, the Middle East, or another market.

A platform may therefore be licensed for certain services in one jurisdiction while being restricted, unavailable, or governed by a different framework elsewhere.

### 2. Concern about customer funds

A crypto exchange holds assets for customers. If the exchange becomes insolvent, is hacked, freezes withdrawals, or faces a major legal issue, customers may not have the same protections available at a traditional bank.

This is why proof of reserves, custody arrangements, withdrawal policies, and corporate disclosures matter.

### 3. Historical compliance problems

OKX’s U.S. legal history is not something a serious review should skip. The U.S. Department of Justice said OKX had an official policy preventing U.S. persons from transacting on its exchange, yet still allowed U.S. customers to access the platform for years. The DOJ also said OKX failed to maintain adequate anti-money-laundering controls during the relevant period.

The company later agreed to criminal forfeiture of approximately $420.3 million and a fine of approximately $84.4 million, with the total penalties exceeding $500 million.

That case concerns historical conduct. It does not automatically mean that every current OKX product is fraudulent. It does mean users should verify the legal entity, availability, and terms that apply to them before depositing funds.

### 4. Complaints about restrictions or support

Crypto exchanges may restrict accounts while investigating identity, source of funds, sanctions exposure, suspicious transactions, or unusual wallet activity. Those controls can be frustrating, especially when a user wants to withdraw immediately.

A compliance review is not proof that an exchange is a scam. It is also not a guarantee that the process will be quick. Users should keep transaction records, complete identity verification honestly, avoid mixing personal and third-party funds, and never rely on an exchange as the only place to store important assets.

### Maker fees and taker fees are different

A **maker** order adds liquidity to the order book, usually through a limit order that does not fill immediately.

A **taker** order removes existing liquidity, such as a market order or a limit order that fills immediately against existing orders.

Taker fees are higher than maker fees in the regular U.S. tier. For example, a regular user may pay a 0.350% taker fee on a standard spot trade, while the maker fee is 0.200%. The exact rate can differ by trading pair and product.

### Futures have additional costs

Futures and perpetual contracts are not priced like ordinary spot purchases. Depending on the product, users may encounter:

- Maker and taker trading fees.
- Funding payments between long and short positions.
- Liquidation charges.
- Expiry or settlement fees.
- Losses caused by leverage and price movement.

OKX explains that funding fees are separate from ordinary trading fees. The funding rate is exchanged between opposing sides of a perpetual contract and may be positive or negative depending on market conditions.

The exchange also warns that deposits below the stated minimum may not be credited automatically and that recovery, when available, can involve an additional fee.

## What are the main risks of using OKX?

### Regulatory and jurisdiction risk

The rules that apply to one OKX user may not apply to another. A feature visible in one country may be blocked in another. An account can also become restricted if the user’s location, identity documents, source of funds, or transaction activity conflicts with the platform’s compliance requirements.

Use the entity and terms that apply to your actual residence. Do not use a VPN to bypass geographic restrictions.

### Custody risk

When assets remain on OKX, they are held through the exchange’s custody system. Proof of reserves can provide useful evidence, but it does not remove custody risk.

For long-term holdings, some users prefer self-custody. That shifts responsibility to the user: losing a seed phrase, signing a malicious transaction, or sending funds to the wrong network can be irreversible.

### Leverage and liquidation risk

Futures and margin products can make a small price movement create a large gain or loss. An account may be liquidated before the user has time to react. New users should understand collateral, maintenance margin, liquidation price, funding, and position sizing before using derivatives.

### Account review risk

A compliance review may delay withdrawals or limit account functions. To reduce avoidable problems, use an account in your legal name, complete KYC accurately, keep records for large transfers, and avoid receiving funds on behalf of other people.

### Phishing and fake support

A legitimate exchange does not make every message claiming to represent it legitimate. Never share a password, two-factor code, seed phrase, or private key with anyone claiming to be OKX support.

The official OKX platform can be imitated by phishing websites, fake mobile apps, social-media accounts, and direct messages. Check the address carefully and access the service through a bookmark or official app store listing.
